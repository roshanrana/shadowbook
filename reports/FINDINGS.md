## Methods

- **Simulator.** `legacy-sim` generates a deterministic transaction stream from
  `SHADOWBOOK_SEED`. Transaction values are a hash of (seed, account, date,
  index) rather than sequential draws from one RNG, so adding or removing a
  business day changes only that day — without this the per-quirk runs would not
  be controlled experiments.
- **Windows.** Quirk cadences are calendar-gated, and a 30-business-day window
  cannot contain both an October holiday and a February leap day. Two windows
  are used: W1 2028-02-28 to 2028-04-07 (30 business days: the leap day, two month ends, and a first-of-month falling on a Saturday) and W2 2028-10-02 to 2028-10-13 (Columbus Day). A guard refuses to run if any enabled
  quirk cannot fire in any configured window.
- **Grains.** Transaction (keyed on transaction id, matched across the whole
  window so a moved posting is timing rather than a missing record), account-day
  (net movement, plus closing ledger and available balances), and control total
  (per currency, per business day). See LLD §3.10.
- **Classification.** Deterministic integer predicates only — no LLM, no learned
  thresholds (FR-R5). Rules: one-minor-unit rounding; scale-100; three day-count
  bases compared by cross-multiplication with a one-denominator-per-side
  tolerance, because both sides rounded once and exact equality never holds; a
  delta equal to a configured fee; a delta equal to one whole movement.
- **Attribution is separate from classification.** The classifier does not know
  quirks exist. Signatures are mapped to candidate quirks afterwards, so the
  reconciler cannot mark its own homework. "Breaks to isolate" is the number of
  breaks carrying a signature before that signature named exactly one quirk.
- **Ingest.** Late, redelivered and truncated extracts are recorded as ingest
  problems and still reconciled; a byte-identical redelivery is discarded rather
  than double counted (FR-R4).

## What this does not prove

- **The quirks are seeded and known.** This calibrates a twin's reconciliation;
  it does not discover unknown behaviour. Every number above answers "how long
  would this have taken to surface", not "what else is in there".
- **A simulator is not a core.** `legacy-sim` is a few thousand lines with
  twelve deliberate divergences. A real incumbent has decades of them, and they
  interact in ways this cannot reproduce.
- **Single-box numbers are not production numbers.** Everything here ran on one
  machine with one instance of each component.
- **Configuration D's guarantee ends at the database boundary.** Kafka
  transactions make the produce and the offset commit atomic with each other;
  they do not extend the guarantee to anything downstream of Postgres.
- **Two calibration limits found by running it.** At small balances a day-count
  basis difference is arithmetically indistinguishable from a rounding
  difference — the interest is too small for the ratio to survive rounding. And
  quirks compound: with all twelve enabled, some become unattributable.
- **Portfolio work, not a production system.** No real money, no real data.
